# Own the audio element, borrow the catalog

Festly can let hosts pick music from a third-party catalog while storing only a reference, but one platform from the host's request works and the other does not. **SoundCloud works when the host pastes a link**: oEmbed needs no API key, uploaders already license embedding on other sites, and Festly stores a permalink, track id, title and artist. **Spotify does not work for this**: its Widget Terms say the Play Button "may not be used for commercial purposes", logged-out guests reportedly hear only 30-second previews, and new developer access is restricted to five users. The bigger constraint is technical, not legal. **Every catalog that only offers an iframe player (SoundCloud, YouTube, Spotify, Deezer) cannot be relied on to start audio from Festly's "Open invitation" tap on iPhone.** WebKit does not pass a parent-page gesture into a cross-origin iframe and has said it does not honor `allow="autoplay"`. The only pattern that starts music reliably from one tap on every phone is a same-document `<audio>` element whose `play()` runs synchronously in the tap handler. So the recommendation has two tiers. Ship **"paste a SoundCloud link"** as the reference-only catalog option, shown as a visible compact player that starts automatically on Android and desktop and asks for one more tap on iOS. Keep **R2-hosted audio** (host uploads plus a small Festly-curated CC BY or royalty-free library) as the default and fallback, because it is the only path where "tap once, music plays while scrolling" is dependable. YouTube, Apple Music, Deezer and Jamendo are ruled out by their terms. Audius and Epidemic Sound are the only catalogs that could feed Festly's own `<audio>` element from a reference, and both need legal or commercial confirmation first.

## The tap only unlocks audio in Festly's own document

Every major engine allows audible playback when `play()` is called during a trusted user gesture in the same document. Without one, `play()` rejects with `NotAllowedError`, and Chrome advises catching that and showing a play button ([Chrome autoplay policy](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/autoplay)). Safari's model is stricter and per element: assume "any use of `<video>` or `<audio>` requires a user gesture click to play", and reuse one element (changing its `src`) rather than creating new ones ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/7734/auto-play-policy-changes-for-macos/)). The activation window is short and not standardized. The HTML spec says only "at most a few seconds" ([HTML spec](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/interaction.html#tracking-user-activation)). Chromium's source sets **5 seconds** ([Chromium source](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/HEAD/third_party/blink/public/common/frame/user_activation_state.h)). WebKit publishes no number ([WebKit User Activation API](https://webkit.org/blog/13862/the-user-activation-api/)). In practice this means no `await`, fetch or deferred state effect may come before `play()`.

Cross-origin players break this chain. Under the spec, activation reaches the activated document, **all ancestor frames and only same-origin descendants**, and postMessage carries no activation ([HTML spec §6.4.2](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/interaction.html#tracking-user-activation)). WebKit states that "cross-origin iframes will not have transient activation" until the user taps inside them ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/13862/the-user-activation-api/)). Chrome offers a way around this: a top frame that has autoplay permission can delegate it with `allow="autoplay"` ([Chrome autoplay policy](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/autoplay)), so on Android Chrome a SoundCloud `widget.play()` or YouTube `playVideo()` sent after the cover tap should work if the player is already loaded. iOS is different. In WebKit bug 190794, which is still open, Apple engineer Jer Noble wrote "we do not honor that attribute" and "I don't anticipate that we will fix this" ([WebKit Bugzilla 190794](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=190794)). The WICG Capability Delegation proposal covers only payment, fullscreen and display capture, not autoplay ([WICG](https://wicg.github.io/capability-delegation/spec.html)). The vendors' own docs agree: YouTube added an `onAutoplayBlocked` event because scripted `playVideo()` can be blocked ([YouTube IFrame API](https://developers.google.com/youtube/iframe_api_reference)), and Spotify warns that `play()` "might not be supported in all browsers and for all users" ([Spotify iFrame API](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/embeds/references/iframe-api)).

The 2018 WebKit statement is the newest explicit one. No 2024–2026 reproduction on iOS 18 or 26 was found, so this conclusion rests on stated policy and older reports. It needs device testing, but Festly should not design on the assumption that it has changed. Background and lock-screen audio on iOS is also unreliable even for a native `<audio>` element: background HTML5 audio broke in iOS 17, was fixed in 17.1, then regressed ([WebKit Bugzilla 261554](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=261554)). An invitation does not need to play from a locked phone, so pausing on `visibilitychange` avoids the problem.

### Spotify fails on three separate grounds

The host asked for Spotify, so the refusal needs to be clear. Legally, the Widget Terms say "the Spotify Play Button may not be used for commercial purposes", require widgets "in the form made available by Spotify, without alteration", and bar obfuscating them ([Spotify Widget Terms](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/embeds/terms)). The Developer Policy separately bars commercial SDAs, syncing recordings with visual media, and business or public playback ([Spotify Developer Policy](https://developer.spotify.com/policy)). On access, apps registered since 27 Nov 2024 lost 30-second preview URLs ([Spotify blog](https://developer.spotify.com/blog/2024-11-27-changes-to-the-web-api)), and since February 2026 Development Mode requires a Premium owner and allows **at most five authorized users** per client ID ([Spotify blog](https://developer.spotify.com/blog/2026-02-06-update-on-developer-access-and-platform-security)). For guests, third-party and community sources report that logged-out listeners get a 30-second preview followed by a login prompt ([Zeroqode](https://docs.zeroqode.com/plugins/spotify-embed-plugin); [Spotify Community](https://community.spotify.com/t5/Other-Podcasts-Partners-etc/Spotify-Play-Button-30-second-limit/td-p/4552931)), which defeats "keeps playing while they scroll". Spotify itself does not document this, so it is the least certain of the three points, but the first two are enough on their own. The most Festly should offer is a plain "Listen on Spotify" link to the host's song, which keyless Spotify oEmbed can enrich with title and artwork ([Spotify oEmbed](https://open.spotify.com/oembed?url=https://open.spotify.com/track/0tgVpDi06FyKpA1z0VMD4v)).

YouTube fails for a different reason. Its policies forbid features that play audio "from a background player" (one "not displayed in the page, tab, or screen that the user is viewing"), forbid separating or isolating the audio of audiovisual content, and require a player of **at least 200×200 px** with nothing overlaid ([YouTube Developer Policies](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/developer-policies); [RMF](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/required-minimum-functionality)). A compliant invitation would carry a 200-pixel video box, and playing a music video as page audio still sits close to the audio-isolation ban. Apple's DPLA limits MusicKit to "facilitating access to Your end users' Apple Music subscriptions" and says its content "cannot be synchronized with any other content" ([Apple DPLA](https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/)). Deezer limits use to "strictly private use within a family scope" with no revenue ([Deezer Terms](https://developers.deezer.com/termsofuse)).

## SoundCloud by pasted link is the defensible reference-only answer

SoundCloud's legal basis is the one Festly needs. Uploaders license "operators and users of any other websites ... to which your Content has been shared or embedded" within the parameters they set, and the player clause forbids only aggregating content into a service that "replicates substantially the offering of the Website", sites dedicated to one artist or genre, and implied endorsement ([SoundCloud Terms of Use](https://soundcloud.com/terms-of-use)). An event invitation with one host-chosen song is none of those. The same clause lets SoundCloud "block your use of the Players and the Widget at any time", which is the reason to keep the use modest and visible.

Use **paste-a-link, not in-app search**. Keyless oEmbed (`https://soundcloud.com/oembed?format=json&url=<permalink>`) returned title, `author_name`, `author_url`, `thumbnail_url` and iframe HTML containing the numeric track id in a live call on 20 Oct 2026 ([oEmbed live call](https://soundcloud.com/oembed?format=json&url=https://soundcloud.com/forss/flickermood)). In-app search would need API keys, which since 18 May 2026 are self-service but **only for Artist Pro subscribers** ([SoundCloud dev blog](https://developers.soundcloud.com/blog/vibe-coding-ai-agent-docs-self-serve-api-keys)). It would also bring in the API Terms, under which commercial use is decided case by case and bars "in-app purchases which allow access to content ... already available via the SoundCloud platform" ([API Terms](https://developers.soundcloud.com/docs/api/terms-of-use)). Two product rules follow from that clause: do not put SoundCloud music behind a paid Festly tier, and do not build a SoundCloud browsing experience. Streaming SoundCloud audio into Festly's own `<audio>` element is not an option at scale either. The `/streams` endpoint now serves HLS AAC ([SoundCloud blog](https://developers.soundcloud.com/blog/api-streaming-urls)) under a **1,500 stream requests per 24 hours** cap ([rate limits](https://developers.soundcloud.com/docs/api/rate-limits)).

Keep the widget visible. No SoundCloud document explicitly forbids a hidden widget, but the API Terms require "clearly visible backlinks" and credit to the uploader and SoundCloud, and forbid interfering with SoundCloud advertising ([API Terms](https://developers.soundcloud.com/docs/api/terms-of-use)). The compact 166-pixel player ([oEmbed docs](https://developers.soundcloud.com/docs/oembed)) works as a docked "now playing" bar. The widget API supports `play`, `pause`, `seekTo` and `setVolume`, plus `READY`, `PLAY`, `FINISH` and `ERROR` events. It has no loop parameter, so loop with `seekTo(0)` and `play()` on `FINISH` ([Widget API](https://developers.soundcloud.com/docs/api/html5-widget)). Two catalog details matter. **Go+ tracks play only 30-second previews in embeds** ([SoundCloud help](https://help.soundcloud.com/hc/en-us/articles/115003448167-Embedding-a-SoundCloud-Go-Track)), and uploaders can disable embedding, after which embed requests fail ([WordPress Trac](https://core.trac.wordpress.org/ticket/46843)). The picker should therefore warn about previews and re-validate links.

On iOS, accept a second tap and design it in. A tap inside the iframe is a real gesture for that frame, so one option is to show the compact player on the cover screen on iPhone and listen for the widget's `PLAY` event to open the invitation ("Tap ▶ to open with music"). This is an inference from the activation rules and needs device testing. The general fallback works everywhere: call `widget.play()` in the cover handler, and if no `PLAY` event arrives within about 1.5 seconds, reveal the player with a "Tap ▶ for music" hint. `setVolume` fades will probably not work on iOS.

## R2 audio is the guaranteed path and the fallback

Host uploads to R2, played through one `<audio>` element, are the only option that meets the full product promise on every device, and they also cover the host who wants a specific commercial song they have rights to. To give hosts choice without third-party risk, add a **small curated Festly library stored in R2**. Incompetech tracks are free with credit under Creative Commons ([Incompetech](https://incompetech.com/music/royalty-free/licenses/)). Pixabay allows free commercial use without attribution, but it has **no music API** and forbids building a "similar or competing service" or bulk copying ([Pixabay Terms](https://pixabay.com/service/terms/)), so keep the set to a hand-picked few dozen tracks with download records kept ([Pixabay FAQ](https://pixabay.com/service/faq/)). AI generators such as Mubert offer sublicensing only on the $499/month tier ([Mubert API](https://mubert.com/api)), which suits a later, larger library.

Two reference-only catalogs that could feed Festly's own `<audio>` element are worth a spike, not a launch. **Audius** search returned JSON with no key, and `/v1/tracks/{id}/stream` redirects to a signed MP3 ([Audius API](https://api.audius.co/v1/tracks/search?query=wedding%20piano&app_name=festly_research)). Its Open Music License grants "Music Players" streaming rights and requires, for commercial use, the artist's name, a copyright notice, an OML notice and a link ([Audius OML](http://audius.org/open-music-license.pdf)). However, the API Terms page could not be read, and in a 50-track sample 21 tracks were labelled "All rights reserved" and 28 had no licence. **Epidemic Sound** advertises a self-serve API with "sublicensing for users" ([Epidemic Sound](https://www.epidemicsound.com/business/developers/)), but whether it permits playback on public web pages, rather than inside rendered videos, and at what price, needs a sales conversation. If either clears, it gives searchable, reference-only music that starts on the cover tap.

## Store a provider-neutral reference plus its licence

Each invitation's music should be one record whose fields work for every provider. Store `provider` (`soundcloud` | `r2_upload` | `festly_library` | `audius` | …), `providerTrackId` (SoundCloud numeric id parsed from the oEmbed iframe `src`, Audius id, or R2 object key), `permalinkUrl` (the canonical public page; this is the attribution backlink), `title`, `artistName`, `artistUrl`, `artworkUrl`, and `durationMs` where known. Add `playback` (`iframe_widget` or `audio_element`) so the guest page picks the player without provider-specific branching. Add the licence fields: `licenseType` (e.g. `soundcloud_embed`, `cc_by_4.0`, `audius_oml`, `host_attested`), `licenseUrl` and a prebuilt `attributionText`. Then add `isPreviewOnly`, `startOffsetSec`, `loop`, `status` (`ok` | `unavailable` | `preview_only`) and `lastValidatedAt`. For uploads, also keep `r2Key`, `mimeType`, `sizeBytes` and a timestamped host rights attestation. Never store resolved stream URLs. Deezer previews and Audius redirects are signed and expire ([Deezer API](https://api.deezer.com/search?q=perfect%20ed%20sheeran&limit=1); [Audius stream](https://api.audius.co/v1/tracks/9bmzA/stream?app_name=festly_research)), and SoundCloud and Jamendo forbid apps designed to cache content ([SoundCloud API Terms](https://developers.soundcloud.com/docs/api/terms-of-use); [Jamendo API Terms](https://devportal.jamendo.com/api_terms_of_use)). Re-run oEmbed when the invitation is published and on a schedule, and when a track becomes unavailable, fall back to the host's chosen default library track instead of playing silence.

### Implementation notes for TanStack Start

Proxy oEmbed calls through a server function so validation, normalization and caching live on the server. On the guest page, render the player at load, not inside the tap handler, because Jer Noble's bug analysis points to iframes and media created asynchronously after the gesture ([WebKit 190794](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=190794)). For `audio_element`, create a single `Audio` with `preload="auto"` and `loop=true`, and call `audio.play()` as the first statement of the button's `onClick`. React's handler runs synchronously inside the trusted event, which should be enough, but do not move `play()` into a `useEffect` that reacts to an "opened" state. Handle rejection with a "Tap to play" chip, keep a persisted mute toggle, pause on `visibilitychange`, and set Media Session title and artwork as an optional extra ([MDN Autoplay guide](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Media/Guides/Autoplay); [dbushell](https://dbushell.com/2023/03/20/ios-pwa-media-session-api/)). Avoid routing the audio through Web Audio, which would need CORS on R2 and follows iOS's silent switch. For `iframe_widget`, load `w.soundcloud.com/player/api.js`, set `allow="autoplay"` on the iframe, wait for `READY` before enabling the button, and use the `PLAY` timeout fallback described above. Show the attribution line near the player in every mode, for example "Music: *Title* by *Artist* on SoundCloud", linking to the permalink.

Several points need confirmation. Device testing is needed for SoundCloud widget start-on-tap on iOS 18 and 26 Safari, Android Chrome, and the in-app browsers of WhatsApp and Instagram, where many invitations will be opened and where no policy source was found. Testing is also needed for the iOS "tap the widget to open" pattern, and for whether ads appear in the embedded SoundCloud widget, which nobody has documented. Legal review is needed for whether a commercial invitation platform's SoundCloud embeds stay within the player clause (written consent from SoundCloud is the only certain route), for the Audius API Terms and how "All rights reserved" labels interact with the OML, for whether Epidemic's API covers public web playback, and for the wording of the host's rights attestation for uploads.

## Conclusion

The host's request comes down to which side of the iframe boundary the audio plays on. Reference-only catalogs are attractive because Festly hosts nothing and the licence comes from the uploader or platform, but the major ones give Festly only a sandboxed player it cannot start on an iPhone and is usually not allowed to hide. Festly should therefore treat "music from SoundCloud" as a visible, credited player feature that sometimes needs a second tap, and present it to hosts that way, rather than promising seamless background music. Seamless background music needs audio Festly can feed into its own element, and today that means R2.

The decision that will matter long term is the provider-neutral track record with explicit licence and attribution fields. With that schema, adding Audius or Epidemic Sound after a legal check becomes a new `provider` value, and the guest player, attribution rendering and re-validation job do not change.
